# Policies and support

## Testing your button on other people

Two of the three assessments ask you to put a working button in front of
someone else and log what happens. Tell that person what you're testing
before they press anything, and get their agreement to be tested. Don't
record or submit anything that identifies them without asking first — what
your submission needs to show is what happened, not who it happened to.

## Testing under a changed condition

Some work asks you to test the same control under a changed condition — a
different reach, no visual acknowledgement, keyboard-only activation. Treat
the changed condition as a property of the one button in front of you, not
as a survey of who a design excludes in general. Log the specific mechanism
that broke — reach, target size, timing window, acknowledgement channel,
activation method — not a general impression that the button was harder to
use.

## Using and crediting someone else's button

Several assessments ask you to name one real button that isn't yours. Say
where you found it: the make and model of a physical control, or the site,
app, and screen for a digital one. If a photo or screenshot isn't one you
took yourself, say whose it is. The dissection is your work; the object
under it usually isn't.

## Submission and deadlines

Each assessment page states its own due date and time. If you expect a
deadline to be difficult to meet, use whatever course or university
extension or support process is available, and do so before the deadline
where possible. Specific penalties for late work are not set by this page —
they follow whatever course- or university-level policy governs this
assessment.

## Academic integrity

Dissect and build your own button, from your own testing. Two students
independently choosing the same real-world button is fine; reusing someone
else's dissection, testing log, or rationale is not. If a way of describing
a button's failure came from a specific source — a person, a paper, a
thread — say where it came from.

## Getting help

For a question about the course itself, email the convenor. For feedback on
work in progress, ask during the session it belongs to rather than by
email — contact details for both are on the [People](/people/) page.
