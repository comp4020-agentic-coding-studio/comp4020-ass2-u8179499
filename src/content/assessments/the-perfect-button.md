Week 1 asked what makes a button feel worth pressing. Every week since has
answered one piece of that question, for one property of one button at a
time. This assessment asks for the whole answer, argued for a single
finished button.

## The brief

> Design one button you are prepared to defend.

Your defence must address:

- wording
- form
- pressability
- feedback
- timing
- consequence
- friction
- behaviour under repeated use
- behaviour for a hand or use condition different from your own
- honesty — whether the button accurately represents what pressing it
  will do

A buttonless alternative may be built and used as a comparison during
development, exactly as Week 12, The Perfect Button, rehearsed — but the
submitted artifact must be a button. The semester does not end with the
conclusion that buttons should disappear.

## What you submit

The working button. Evidence from testing it. A concise written defence of
the design decisions, with explicit reference to named weeks: Week 4 for
the promise it makes, Week 5 for its timing, Week 7 for how you know it
doesn't silently fail, Week 9 for how it holds up in a hand unlike your
own, Week 10 for what friction guards its consequence, and Week 11 for why
it isn't lying to whoever presses it.

## Marking

Holistic: judged as a whole defence of the button against the ten
properties above, not as a checklist. A submission that argues all ten
honestly, including where the button falls short, is stronger than one
that claims success on all ten without evidence.
