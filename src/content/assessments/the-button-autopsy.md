The course's first four weeks gave you the vocabulary this assessment asks
you to use on a button you didn't build. Before Week 5 asks you to
construct anything, this assessment asks you to take one apart.

## The brief

> Choose one real button. Take it apart in writing before you're allowed to
> build one.

Pick one button — physical or digital, yours or someone else's, but one
specific object, not a category of button. Dissect it:

- what action it appears to promise
- what physical, visual, or conventional signifiers make it read as
  pressable
- what earlier button convention it inherits
- what actually happens after the press
- whether the apparent importance of the button matches the consequence

Each point draws on a specific week: the promise from Week 4, What the
Button Promises; the signifiers from Week 3, The Grammar of Pressable; the
inherited convention from Week 1, The Button Before the Screen, and Week 2,
Translating the Push; and the match, or mismatch, between apparent and
actual weight from all four at once.

## What you submit

A concise, annotated document — labelled notes against a photo or
screenshot of the button, not a generic essay about buttons in general.
Every claim should point at the specific object in front of you, not at
buttons as a category.

End with one defensible claim: state plainly whether this button works,
fails, or misrepresents itself, and say why, in terms of the dissection
above.

## Marking

Weighted, against how accurately you've dissected the five points against
the actual object, and how well the final claim follows from that
dissection rather than from a general opinion about interface design.
