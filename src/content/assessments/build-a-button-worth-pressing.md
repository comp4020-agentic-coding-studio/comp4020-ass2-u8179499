The Button Autopsy took someone else's button apart. This assessment asks
you to build one that survives the same kind of scrutiny — including from
you, once you've tested it on someone else.

## The brief

> Design and build one working digital button.

It must deliberately address:

- pressability and signifiers, from Week 3, The Grammar of Pressable
- the relationship between consequence and feedback, from Week 4, What the
  Button Promises
- feedback timing, from Week 5, Feedback and the Length of a Second
- repeat pressing or reward, where relevant, from Week 6, The Button That
  Wants You Back
- at least one tested point where the same control fails under a changed
  use condition, from Week 9, One Button, Many Hands

Not every button needs every property at equal weight — one with no
meaningful repeat-press behaviour doesn't need to invent one for Week 6's
sake — but each item above should be visibly answered somewhere in what
you submit, not left implicit.

## What you submit

The working button. A record of testing it with another person: what you
asked them to do, what happened, and where the changed-condition test
broke it. A short rationale — a page, not a report — that argues for your
decisions using the course's vocabulary, not generic design language like
"intuitive" or "user-friendly."

## Marking

Weighted, across the button's own construction, the quality and honesty of
your testing evidence, and whether the rationale argues from the course
rather than restating it.
