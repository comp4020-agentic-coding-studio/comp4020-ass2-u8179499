The course opened with one question: what makes a button feel worth
pressing? Twelve weeks have answered pieces of it without stating the
whole thing at once. This week asks for the whole thing.

## What the semester actually gave you

- a physical ancestry, from Weeks 1 and 2: what a button is before and
  after it becomes a rectangle on glass
- pressability, from Week 3: the difference between looking pressable and
  being pressable
- a promise, from Week 4: what state change the button is actually
  claiming
- a length, from Week 5: how long silence can last before the promise
  reads as broken
- a reward, from Week 6: what makes a button worth pressing again
- a failure, from Week 7: what a button teaches you once it stops working
- a habit, from Week 8: what a button becomes once it stops being looked
  at
- a hand, from Week 9: whether the same mechanism holds for someone
  different from whoever built it
- a friction, from Week 10: what should stand between a press and its
  consequence
- an honesty, from Week 11: what a button owes the person pressing it

None of these are independent checklist items. A button that satisfies
all ten and still isn't worth pressing is a button that got the
individual pieces right and the whole thing wrong.

## The strongest button you built

Choose the single strongest button you produced this semester — from any
week's log, prototype, or redesign. Say which week it came from and why
you're choosing it over the others.

## One buttonless comparison

Build one buttonless alternative that accomplishes the same action — a
gesture, a voice command, an automatic trigger, whatever removes the
press entirely. Build this only to find out what the button was
contributing. This is a comparison, not a replacement: the semester does
not end with the conclusion that buttons should disappear.

Note specifically what the buttonless version lost — acknowledgement, a
deliberate moment before an irreversible action, something else you can
name.

## The final button, defended

Produce a final version of your chosen button, revised in light of the
comparison. Then write its defence: name at least four specific ideas
from Weeks 1–11 it satisfies, and state, in your own words, why this
button earns the press it asks for.

This defence is the direct rehearsal for The Perfect Button, the
semester's final assessment. The assessment brief itself is not part of
this session.

Twelve weeks ago the course asked what makes a button feel worth
pressing. Your defence is the answer, argued for one specific button
instead of all of them.
