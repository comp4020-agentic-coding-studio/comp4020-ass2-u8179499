A doorbell does not open a door. A doorbell closes a circuit in a box on the
porch, which is wired to a chime or a buzzer somewhere else in the building,
which produces a sound that a person eventually hears and acts on. Nobody
presses a doorbell to make a sound. They press it to summon a person who is
not currently in the room.

That gap — between the small thing you do with your hand and the much larger
thing that happens somewhere you can't see — is the button's whole job. A
button is a device that translates a small human action into a remote
consequence. Everything else about a button, including every button that
comes up for the rest of this course, is a variation on that one fact.

## Four buttons, before pixels

- **The doorbell.** Your press does not ring the bell. It closes a circuit;
  the ringing is downstream of that.
- **The elevator call button.** Your press does not move the car. It
  registers a request with a controller that is already deciding where
  every car in the shaft should go next.
- **The pedestrian crossing button.** Your press does not change the light.
  In most installations it registers a request that a signal controller
  may or may not act on sooner than it would have anyway.
- **The panic button.** Your press does not stop the danger. It sends a
  signal to a person or a system whose job is to respond to that signal.

In every case, the button is not the thing that solves the problem. It is
the thing that tells something else a problem exists.

## The log

Pick three physical buttons you actually pressed today — not hypothetical
ones, the ones your hand touched. For each, write down:

1. what the button is called, or what you'd call it if it has no name
2. where it physically is
3. what happens immediately at the button (a click, a light, nothing)
4. what happens remotely, as a direct result of your press, that would not
   have happened otherwise

If you can't find out what happens remotely for one of your three, say so —
don't invent it. Not knowing what a button does on the other end is itself
worth logging.

Submit the three entries before next session. Session 2 opens with one of
yours, picked at random.
