Week 3 asked whether a shape earns the right to look pressable. This week
asks a different question about the same object: once a shape has earned
that right, what is it actually claiming will happen if you press it?

Every button is a promise about a state change. "Like" claims a small,
reversible change to a counter and a record somewhere that you did it.
"Send" claims a message will leave your control and arrive somewhere you can
no longer edit it. "Delete account" claims something closer to the opposite
of "like" — a state that, depending on the application, may not come back.

## The axis

Reversible sits at one end: pressing again, or pressing undo, restores the
previous state at no real cost. Irreversible sits at the other: once
pressed, the previous state is permanently unavailable, or recoverable only
at a cost the button gave no warning of.

Most buttons sit somewhere between the two, and where they sit is not always
visible in how they look. A button styled in quiet grey text can trigger
something irreversible; a button styled in alarming red can trigger
something you can undo in one click. The same mismatch Week 3 diagnosed
between a shape and its behaviour shows up here between a button's weight
and its consequence.

## Five buttons, one application

Pick one real application you use — pick the app, not five buttons from five
different places. Choose five of its buttons and, for each:

1. name the button as it's labelled
2. say in one sentence what state actually changes when it's pressed
3. place it on the reversible-to-irreversible axis, and say what
   "reversing" it would actually require, if anything
4. note its visual weight — is it styled to match how consequential it is,
   understated, or overstated?

## The mismatch

Somewhere in your five, or elsewhere in the same application, find at least
one button whose visual or verbal presentation misrepresents its actual
consequence — quieter than it should be, louder than it needs to be, or
worded to sound reversible when it isn't. Name the button, the mismatch, and
which direction it runs.
