# The Grammar of Pressable

How does a shape learn to say "press me"?

---

## Before buttons had labels

<div class="stage">
  <div class="physical-btn"></div>
</div>

A raised circle on a panel. No word printed on it. Four things already tell
a finger what to do: its shape, how far it travels, how hard it resists,
where it sits relative to a hand.

---

## Looks pressable

<div class="stage">
  <div class="ui-frame">
    <div class="ui-btn">Confirm</div>
  </div>
</div>

Border. Depth. A contained shape. A label naming the action. Four
signifiers, stacked on one rectangle.

---

## Looks pressable, but isn't

<div class="stage">
  <div class="ui-frame">
    <div class="ui-btn">Draft saved</div>
  </div>
</div>

Same border, same depth, same shape. This one does nothing. It is a status
message wearing a button's clothes.

---

## Is pressable, but doesn't look it

<div class="ui-frame" style="text-align: left;">
  <p class="ui-prose">
    Your session is about to expire. <span class="ui-inline-link">Stay
    signed in</span> to keep working.
  </p>
</div>

No border. No depth. No colour change. One of those words is a working
control; the rest are prose.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Affordance

<div class="stage">
  <div class="physical-btn"></div>
</div>

The raised circle invites a fingertip because of its curve, not because
anyone was told what it does. That is affordance: what an object suggests
about itself, before signage or history get involved.

---

## Signifier

<div class="stage">
  <div class="ui-frame">
    <div class="ui-btn">Confirm</div>
  </div>
</div>

The border and the shadow are not the button. They are a signifier: a
signal placed on the button, telling a flat rectangle to read as raised.
A doorbell's actual raised rim does the same job with physics instead of
a shadow.

---

## Convention

<div class="shape-row">
  <div class="shape shape--rounded">Save</div>
  <div class="shape shape--pill">&#10003;</div>
  <div class="physical-btn physical-btn--small"></div>
</div>

Different shapes. Same expectation.

That learned agreement is convention — doing work that affordance and
signifier alone can't.

---

## Invention

<div class="shape-row">
  <div class="shape shape--square">Go</div>
  <div class="shape shape--rounded">Go</div>
  <div class="shape shape--pill">Go</div>
  <div class="shape shape--blob">Go</div>
</div>

Each shape departs a little further from the last. Somewhere along this
row a shape stops reading as a button at all. Convention has a breaking
point; invention is what happens on the far side of it.

## Week 3 activity

Find one real interface element that is mis-clickable or visually
ambiguous. Name which convention it breaks: affordance, signifier, or an
established convention. Propose the smallest change that would make its
pressability honest — not a redesign, the smallest change.
